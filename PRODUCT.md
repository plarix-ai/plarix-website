# Plarix — Product truth

## What the company is

Plarix is an AI integration company. We build **agentic process automation** for home services:
operational AI that runs real back-office processes end to end, engineered from the business
process first rather than from a model or a chat interface.

We are not a chatbot vendor, not a receptionist, not a field-service platform. We do not replace
the system a shop already runs on. We sit beside it and take over the work it generates.

## Who we sell to

Owner-operated HVAC, plumbing, electrical and adjacent home services companies. Roughly 20 to 100
people on payroll, $3M to $25M in revenue. The owner came up through the trade. He has been burned
by software before: long onboarding, quiet support, a migration that broke something. He is not
anti-technology. He is anti-getting-burned-again.

He does not want another screen to check. He wants the work to stop being his problem.

## The wedge vs. the company

**The wedge** (first process, most accounts): manufacturer warranty claims, parts credits, labor
reimbursement and rebates. Chosen because the result is a number that either exists or does not,
which makes it the fastest place to prove the model.

**The company**: the operational layer that runs the processes a home services business generates
after the job is done. Warranty and rebate recovery, invoice and job reconciliation, purchasing and
vendor credits, permits and compliance, technician pay and commissions, membership renewals.

The website must read as the company, not as the wedge. Warranty appears as the honest starting
point, never as the category.

## Competitive frame

Nearly every AI vendor in home services sells the **front office**: AI receptionists, voice agents,
booking bots, call scoring. "Never miss a call." That market is loud and undifferentiated.

Plarix sells the **back office** — everything after the call. This is both true and unclaimed.

ServiceTitan and its peers are not villains and not competitors. They run the business. We run the
work the business produces. Different job.

## How we work (three verbs, in order)

1. **Count** — read the real data first. Jobs, invoices, parts, payouts. Hand over a real count of
   what is sitting there before anything is sold.
2. **Build** — build the narrow thing that handles exactly what was found. Runs inside existing
   systems. Human in the loop when real money or reputation is on the line.
3. **Prove** — one page. What ran, what it recovered, what it saved. Before and after, in dollars.

## Pricing shape (publishable; no invented dollar figures)

Audit free. Build fee scoped to what the audit actually found. Small monthly to keep it running.
No long contract, cancel anytime. The comparison we ask the owner to make is to a hire
($45K–$110K/yr fully loaded coordinator), never to a software subscription.

## Promises that ship publicly

- No long contract.
- Nobody on the team gets replaced. We automate paperwork, not people.
- No recording, scoring, or surveillance of technicians. Ever.
- No number promised that has not been proven on the customer's own data.
- No data migration, no pricebook rebuild, no retraining. We read what is already there.
- We never become the platform. Whatever a shop runs on, we sit beside it.

## Verified facts available for use

- 250–500 open "warranty coordinator / claims administrator" postings nationally, $45K–$110K fully
  loaded.
- 100,000+ unfilled HVAC technician jobs nationally.
- ServiceTitan public pricing range: $245–$500 per technician per month, $5K–$50K implementation,
  12–36 month contracts.
- WarrCloud (same model, car dealerships): $40M raised, 500+ dealers.

**Never publish**: the "12–18% of eligible claims go unrecovered" figure. It is an unproven vendor
hypothesis, not a promise.

## Voice

Short sentences. Real trade vocabulary. A specific number before an opinion. Respect the owner.
Understate rather than oversell. No em dashes. No "AI-powered", "agentic", "seamless",
"revolutionize", "unlock", "cutting-edge", "we're excited to announce". Say what it actually does.

The word "AI" appears rarely and only where it is the plainest available word. Never as the pitch.
